# AI Learning Aid: A Bill to Modernize and Extend AGOA to Increase Trade Between the United States and Africa

## Summary
The African Growth and Opportunity Act (AGOA, 2000) lets eligible sub-Saharan African countries export thousands of products to the U.S. duty-free, as long as they meet conditions like rule of law, market reforms and human rights. This bill extends AGOA through December 31, 2041, creates a $250 million a year Trade Capacity Initiative run by the U.S. Trade Representative (USTR) to help small businesses, manufacturing and customs systems, keeps the annual eligibility review, and gives countries a written notice and up to 180 days to fix a problem before USTR recommends removing them (except for severe human rights violations or national security threats). USTR reports to Congress every year.

Timeline you need: AGOA lapsed on September 30, 2025, was briefly extended in early 2026, and in September 2026 President Trump signed a two-year extension through the end of 2028 "without substantive policy changes" (allAfrica). So this bill isn't rescuing AGOA from expiring today; it's choosing a 13-year extension and new spending over short-term renewals.

The numbers: 32 of 49 possible countries currently qualify. More than $8 billion in African exports entered the U.S. under AGOA in 2024; crude oil is about 25%. South Africa and Nigeria are the top exporters. Meanwhile China exported $141 billion to Africa in just the first eight months of 2025 (Council on Foreign Relations). The clash: long-term stability and competing with China versus keeping leverage and spending money on a program whose benefits are concentrated in a few countries.

## Affirmative arguments
### Short renewals kill the long-term investment AGOA is supposed to create
Warrant: A factory owner deciding whether to build an apparel plant in Kenya or Lesotho needs to know duty-free access will last longer than the payback period. AGOA has lurched from lapse to one-year patch to two-year patch since 2025. When Lesotho's access was uncertain, a World Bank estimate projected a 16% drop in its apparel and textile exports (CFR). A 2041 date gives investors a timeline they can build factories on.
Impact: Jobs in some of the poorest countries on earth. Across beneficiaries, 75 to 90% of AGOA apparel jobs went to low-income women (CFR).
They'll say: "Congress just extended it to 2028."
Respond: Two years is shorter than it takes to plan, build and staff a factory. That's why investors keep waiting.
Best speech slot: Authorship.

### Trade with Africa is a strategic contest with China, and the U.S. is losing
Warrant: China became Africa's largest trade partner in 2009 and exported $141 billion to the continent in just the first eight months of 2025, on track to exceed $200 billion for the year. Sub-Saharan Africa is nearly 4% of China's imports but under 1% of U.S. imports (CFR). Africa will be home to a quarter of the world's people by mid-century. AGOA is the main U.S. tool for building economic ties there.
Impact: Countries that trade with China vote with China at the UN and give it ports, minerals and influence. Long-term AGOA keeps the U.S. in the game.
They'll say: "AGOA is too small to matter against China."
Respond: Then this is the moment to grow it, which is what the $250 million capacity initiative does.
Best speech slot: Early or mid affirmative.

### The capacity initiative fixes AGOA's biggest weakness: most countries barely use it
Warrant: Angola, Ghana, Kenya, Madagascar and South Africa supplied nearly 90% of non-energy U.S. imports from Africa in 2022 (CFR). Many eligible countries can't meet U.S. standards or move goods through ports efficiently. The bill directs USTR to "prioritize countries and industries that have historically underutilized AGOA preferences," and to report annually on results.
Impact: Turns the negative's "benefits are concentrated" argument into a reason to vote affirmative.
Best speech slot: Mid-round affirmative.

## Negative arguments
### Locking AGOA in until 2041 gives up the leverage that makes it work
Warrant: AGOA's power comes from conditions: countries meet rule-of-law and human rights standards to keep access. Short renewals let Congress revisit those standards and respond to coups and abuses. Seventeen of 49 countries are currently ineligible, including Burkina Faso, Ethiopia and Mali (CFR). The bill adds a mandatory 180-day correction window before termination for most violations, which slows the U.S. response.
Impact: Less pressure for reform and slower action against governments that backslide.
They'll say: "The 180-day window doesn't apply to severe human rights violations."
Respond: It applies to everything else, including election fraud, corruption and attacks on independent courts, which is how democracy usually erodes.
Best speech slot: First negative.

### The benefits flow mostly to oil and a few middle-income countries
Warrant: Crude oil is about 25% of AGOA imports, and South Africa alone supplied over 56% of non-energy U.S. imports from Africa in 2021 (CFR). South Africa is one of Africa's richest economies. A preference program meant to fight poverty mostly benefits industries and countries that would trade anyway.
Impact: U.S. taxpayers and workers give up tariff revenue for limited development gains.
They'll say: "That's why the bill adds the capacity initiative."
Respond: $250 million a year spread across 32 countries is under $8 million per country. It's not enough to change who benefits.
Best speech slot: Early or mid negative.

### Congress just renewed AGOA, so this spends $250 million a year to fix a problem that isn't urgent
Warrant: The September 2026 extension already restored AGOA through 2028, with retroactive refunds for duties paid during the lapse (allAfrica). The bill adds $3.75 billion over 15 years in new spending, starting while AGOA is already in effect. Congress can debate long-term reform in 2028 with better data on how the program performs under current tariffs.
Impact: Good for crystallization: the negative supports trade with Africa and still opposes this bill as unnecessary and expensive.
Best speech slot: Mid or late negative.

## Likely questions
### When does AGOA expire under current law?
- For: either
Ask as: Negative, to the author.
Then ask: So AGOA is in effect right now?
Then ask: Then what's the urgency for a 13-year extension and new spending?
Goal: Remove the "save AGOA" frame early.
Best answer (if you're affirmative): "December 2028, after a year-long lapse and two short patches. That uncertainty is the problem. No investor builds a factory on a two-year promise."

### How much of the $250 million goes to each eligible country?
- For: either
Ask as: Negative.
Best answer (if you're affirmative): "It isn't divided evenly; it targets the countries and industries that use AGOA least, which is where small investments in customs and standards make the biggest difference."

### How does the 180-day notice affect the U.S. response to a coup?
- For: either
Ask as: Negative.
Best answer (if you're affirmative): "A coup is a threat to democracy and almost always involves serious human rights abuses, which skip the 180-day window. The window is for fixable problems, like a customs rule, not for coups."

### How much did China export to Africa in the first eight months of 2025?
- For: either
Ask as: Affirmative, friendly question to the author.
Best answer (if you're the author): "$141 billion, on track to pass $200 billion for the year, according to the Council on Foreign Relations. Africa is nearly 4% of China's imports and under 1% of ours."

### Is South Africa, one of Africa's richest economies, the biggest beneficiary?
- For: either
Ask as: Negative.
Best answer (if you're affirmative): "It's the largest non-energy exporter, yes. That's exactly why the bill tells USTR to prioritize underused countries, so the benefits spread."

### If AGOA lapses again, what happens to apparel workers in Lesotho?
- For: either
Ask as: Affirmative, to a negative speaker.
Goal: Make the human cost of short renewals concrete.
Best answer (if you're negative): "That's why Congress renewed it through 2028. The answer to uncertainty is timely renewals, not locking in policy until 2041."
