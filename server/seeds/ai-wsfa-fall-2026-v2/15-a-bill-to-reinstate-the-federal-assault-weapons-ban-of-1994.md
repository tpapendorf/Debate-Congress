# AI Learning Aid: A Bill to Reinstate the Federal Assault Weapons Ban of 1994

## Summary
The bill reinstates the 1994 federal ban (the Public Safety and Recreational Firearms Use Protection Act) on manufacturing, transferring or possessing a "semiautomatic assault weapon," using that law's definition. The ATF enforces it starting June 1, 2027. The original ban ran from 1994 until it expired in 2004.

Read Section 1 against the 1994 law, because the differences decide this debate. The 1994 law had two main parts: a ban on listed semiautomatic weapons with certain military-style features, and a ban on new large-capacity magazines (more than 10 rounds). This bill only mentions the weapons. It also bans "possession," while the 1994 law grandfathered guns people already owned legally. The industry group NSSF estimates there are now about 32 million AR-style "modern sporting rifles" in circulation, so whether existing guns are grandfathered is a huge question the text doesn't answer. Section 2 also cites "Section B" of the 1994 Act, which isn't how that law was organized; the definition lived in 18 U.S.C. § 921(a)(30).

The research is mixed. Criminologist Christopher Koper found the large-capacity magazine limits were the law's "most important provisions" and could reduce mass shooting deaths by 11 to 15% as an upper bound, while RAND calls the evidence on assault weapon bans and mass shootings "inconclusive." Since 2022, courts also apply the Supreme Court's Bruen test, which asks whether a gun law fits the nation's historical tradition. The clash: mass shooting lethality versus constitutional rights and whether this text works.

## Affirmative arguments
### These weapons make mass shootings deadlier
Warrant: Semiautomatic rifles with military-style features fire as fast as the shooter can pull the trigger and are designed for rapid, accurate fire at a distance. After the ban expired in 2004, criminologist Grant Duwe found mass public shootings became more lethal (FactCheck.org). The worst mass shootings of the last two decades, like Parkland, Uvalde and Las Vegas, involved these weapons.
Impact: Fewer victims per attack. Every person who survives because a shooter had a less lethal weapon is the impact.
They'll say: "Research shows the ban didn't reduce mass shootings."
Respond: The goal isn't only fewer shootings; it's fewer deaths when one happens. Duwe's finding is about lethality, which is what the ban targets.
Best speech slot: Authorship.

### Crimes with banned weapons fell after the ban took effect
Warrant: Koper's studies found crimes with assault weapons began declining shortly after 1994 (FactCheck.org). The ban worked slowly because it grandfathered millions of existing weapons and magazines, but the direction was clear: fewer new weapons in circulation meant fewer used in crimes.
Impact: Shows the ban works over time, which is why reinstating it now protects the next generation.
They'll say: "Overall gun crime with other semiautomatics stayed steady."
Respond: That's why the ban's goal is narrow: the weapons used in the deadliest attacks. A law doesn't fail because it doesn't solve every problem.
Best speech slot: Early affirmative.

### The public supports it and many states already do it
Warrant: Washington banned the sale of many assault weapons in 2023, and several other states, including California, New York and Illinois, have similar laws. A federal rule stops guns from flowing in from states without bans.
Impact: State laws only work as well as the weakest neighboring state. A national floor makes every state's law effective.
Best speech slot: Mid-round affirmative.

## Negative arguments
### The bill leaves out the part of the 1994 law that experts say mattered most
Warrant: Koper, the leading researcher on the 1994 ban, called the large-capacity magazine limits its "most important provisions" and estimated they could cut mass shooting deaths by 11 to 15% at most (FactCheck.org). Section 1 of this bill only reinstates the ban on "semiautomatic assault weapons." It never mentions magazines. An earlier Justice Department-funded study found the ban's success in reducing crimes with banned guns was "mixed."
Impact: The affirmative gets the political fight and the legal risk of a ban, without the provision most likely to save lives.
They'll say: "Section 1 reinstates 'the provisions' of the Act."
Respond: It says the provisions will be reinstated "to ban the manufacturing, transfer, or possession of a semiautomatic assault weapon." That's the scope. Ask the author to find the word "magazine" in the bill.
Best speech slot: First negative.

### Banning "possession" of 32 million rifles is unenforceable and likely unconstitutional
Warrant: NSSF estimates about 32 million AR-style rifles are in circulation. The 1994 law grandfathered existing guns; this bill bans possession outright, with no grandfather clause in its text. That would make millions of law-abiding owners criminals overnight, with no buyback or registration plan. Under Bruen (2022) and Heller (2008), courts ask whether a gun law fits historical tradition and whether a weapon is "in common use," and 32 million rifles is a strong common-use argument.
Impact: Either mass noncompliance and selective enforcement, or a law struck down in court, which helps no one.
They'll say: "Reinstating the 1994 Act brings back its grandfather clause."
Respond: Then the bill's own words, "ban the possession," contradict it. If the author meant to grandfather existing guns, the bill should say so.
Best speech slot: Early or mid negative.

### The research doesn't show the ban reduced mass shootings
Warrant: RAND's review of gun policy research found "inconclusive evidence for the effect of assault weapon bans on mass shootings," and lead author Andrew Morral said the research "doesn't provide compelling evidence one way or the other." A 2020 study found these bans "do not seem to be associated with the incidence of fatal mass shootings" (FactCheck.org). Rifles of all types are used in a small share of gun homicides; most involve handguns.
Impact: The bill imposes a major restriction on a constitutional right without clear evidence of the benefit.
Best speech slot: Mid or late negative. Good for crystallization.

## Likely questions
### Does this bill ban large-capacity magazines?
- For: either
Ask as: Negative, to the author.
Then ask: The researcher you'd cite called magazine limits the most important part of the 1994 law, correct?
Then ask: So your bill leaves out the most effective part?
Goal: The strongest line in the debate. Stay calm and let the text speak.
Best answer (if you're affirmative): "Section 1 reinstates 'the provisions' of the 1994 Act, which includes its magazine limits. If the wording needs to be clearer, a friendly amendment fixes it. The policy is the full 1994 law."

### Does this bill let people keep rifles they already legally own?
- For: either
Ask as: Negative.
Then ask: Then what happens to 32 million rifles on June 1, 2027?
Best answer (if you're affirmative): "It reinstates the 1994 Act, which grandfathered existing weapons. The goal is to stop new sales, not to take guns from current owners."

### Did mass shootings become deadlier after the ban expired in 2004?
- For: either
Ask as: Affirmative, to any negative speaker.
Goal: Get Duwe's finding on the record.
Best answer (if you're negative): "Some research suggests that, but the same researcher didn't reach a definitive conclusion about why, and RAND calls the evidence inconclusive."

### How does this bill pass the Bruen test?
- For: either
Ask as: Negative.
Best answer (if you're affirmative): "Several federal appeals courts have upheld state assault weapons bans after Bruen, finding a historical tradition of restricting especially dangerous weapons. And the Supreme Court has said the Second Amendment isn't unlimited."

### What share of gun murders involve rifles of any kind?
- For: either
Ask as: Negative.
Goal: Shift the debate from mass shootings to overall gun violence, where handguns dominate.
Best answer (if you're affirmative): "Small, and we're not claiming otherwise. This bill targets the weapons that turn a shooting into a massacre."

### What does "Section B" of the 1994 Act refer to?
- For: either
Ask as: Negative, a quick drafting point.
Best answer (if you're affirmative): "The definition section of the Act, codified at 18 U.S.C. § 921(a)(30). The intent is to use the exact definition from 1994."
