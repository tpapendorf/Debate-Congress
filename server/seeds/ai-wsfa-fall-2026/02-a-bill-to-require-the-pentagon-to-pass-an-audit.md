# AI Learning Aid: A Bill to Require the Pentagon to Pass an Audit

## Summary
Cuts the budget of any Pentagon component 10% for each year it fails to get a clean audit opinion, starting July 1, 2027. The withheld money goes back to the Treasury, with the DoD Inspector General and GAO overseeing it.

## Affirmative arguments
### The Pentagon has never passed a full audit
Warrant: It has failed every audit since the first department-wide audit in 2018.
Impact: Taxpayers can't be sure where hundreds of billions of dollars go.

### Real consequences create urgency
Warrant: Deadlines without penalties have slipped for decades.
Impact: A budget penalty gives leaders a reason to fix accounting fast.

### Better books mean better readiness
Warrant: Audits find lost equipment, inventory errors and waste.
Impact: Money saved can go to troops and real needs.

## Negative arguments
### Cutting budgets could hurt troops and readiness
Warrant: A 10% cut lands on operations and people, not just accountants.
Impact: National security could suffer while the books get fixed.

### Cuts make the audit harder to pass
Warrant: Fixing accounting systems costs money, and cutting budgets removes the funds needed to fix them.
Impact: It could trap agencies in a cycle of failure.

### Progress is already happening
Warrant: Defense leaders have set a goal of a clean audit by 2028, and some components already pass.
Impact: Penalties may disrupt reforms that are working.

### How do you fix accounting systems with 10% less money?
- For: aff

- For: neg
Answer: Deadlines have been missed for decades; neg should argue penalties backfire.

### What does a clean audit opinion actually mean?
- For: either
