# AI Learning Aid: A Bill to Reinstate the Federal Assault Weapons Ban of 1994

## Summary
Reinstates the 1994 federal assault weapons ban, prohibiting the manufacture, transfer or possession of semiautomatic assault weapons as defined in that law. The ATF enforces it starting June 1, 2027.

## Affirmative arguments
### Assault weapons are used in many of the deadliest mass shootings
Warrant: Their high rate of fire and capacity increase casualties.
Impact: Limiting access can reduce deaths in attacks.

### Some research found fewer mass shooting deaths during the ban
Warrant: Studies comparing 1994–2004 to other periods found lower fatalities.
Impact: Reinstating it could save lives.

### Most Americans support restrictions
Warrant: Polls consistently show majority support for limits on these weapons.
Impact: Law should reflect public will.

## Negative arguments
### The evidence is inconclusive
Warrant: RAND's review found limited or inconclusive evidence on assault weapon bans.
Impact: The law may not reduce violence.

### Most gun deaths involve handguns
Warrant: Rifles of all kinds account for a small share of gun homicides.
Impact: The ban targets a small part of the problem.

### It raises Second Amendment concerns
Warrant: Courts have been reconsidering gun laws under newer Supreme Court standards.
Impact: The ban could be struck down.

## Likely questions
### What counts as an 'assault weapon' under the 1994 definition?
- For: aff
Answer: Semiautomatic firearms with certain military-style features, listed in the law.

### Does the ban take guns from people who already own them?
- For: aff
Answer: The bill bans possession, so be ready to explain grandfathering or buybacks.

### Why ban rifles when handguns cause most deaths?
- For: aff
Answer: These weapons cause the most deaths in mass shootings.

### Would this survive the Supreme Court's Bruen test?
- For: either
Answer: Uncertain; aff should cite history of regulating dangerous weapons.
