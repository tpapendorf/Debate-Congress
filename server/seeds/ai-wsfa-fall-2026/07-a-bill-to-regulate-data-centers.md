# AI Learning Aid: A Bill to Regulate Data Centers

## Summary
Sets rules for building data centers: within 2 miles of a populated area, no drinking water for cooling, a 200,000 square foot size cap, proof of no harm to wildlife, at least 200 jobs for 12 months, and state approval. Violations can be fined up to $400 million.

## Affirmative arguments
### Data centers strain local water supplies
Warrant: Many cool their servers with large amounts of water, sometimes drinking water.
Impact: Communities in dry regions face shortages.

### Communities deserve a say
Warrant: Large data centers raise electricity demand and change local land use.
Impact: State approval makes sure the public benefits.

### Jobs requirements protect taxpayers
Warrant: States often give big tax breaks for few permanent jobs.
Impact: Requiring 200 jobs ensures communities get something back.

## Negative arguments
### The jobs rule would block nearly all data centers
Warrant: Data centers employ relatively few permanent workers once built.
Impact: The U.S. would lose AI and cloud investment to other countries.

### A size cap conflicts with efficiency
Warrant: Larger campuses can be more energy- and water-efficient per server.
Impact: Smaller, scattered centers could increase total resource use.

### States already regulate land and water
Warrant: Zoning and water permits are state and local powers.
Impact: A federal rule adds confusion and may face legal challenges.

## Likely questions
### How many existing data centers employ 200 people?
- For: aff
Answer: Be ready to admit few do, and argue the rule targets new subsidized projects.

### Why require data centers to be near populated areas?
- For: either
Answer: Likely so communities can see and weigh the impact; neg can argue it's backwards for noise and power.

### What does cooling look like without drinking water?
- For: aff
Answer: Recycled water, air cooling or closed-loop systems.

### Does Congress have the power to control where states allow buildings?
- For: aff
Answer: Through interstate commerce, since data centers serve national markets.
