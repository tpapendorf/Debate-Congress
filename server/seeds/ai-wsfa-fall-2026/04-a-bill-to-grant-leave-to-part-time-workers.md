# AI Learning Aid: A Bill to Grant Leave to Part-Time Workers

## Summary
Lowers the Family and Medical Leave Act eligibility rule from 12 months and 1,250 hours of work to 90 days of service, so more part-time and newer workers can take job-protected unpaid leave. The Department of Labor would carry it out within six months.

## Affirmative arguments
### Many workers are left out of FMLA today
Warrant: The 12-month and 1,250-hour rules exclude many part-time, seasonal and newer workers.
Impact: They can lose their jobs when they or a family member gets sick.

### Part-time workers are often those who need protection most
Warrant: Lower-wage workers are more likely to be part-time and less likely to have paid leave.
Impact: Job protection keeps families financially stable.

### Health emergencies don't wait a year
Warrant: A serious illness or new baby can come in a worker's first months on the job.
Impact: Leave protection should match real life.

## Negative arguments
### Small businesses face higher costs and disruption
Warrant: Employers must hold jobs open and cover shifts for newer staff.
Impact: Some may hire fewer workers or cut hours.

### 90 days is too short to build trust
Warrant: Employers may be reluctant to hire if a new worker can take 12 weeks of leave soon after starting.
Impact: Workers could face more hiring discrimination.

### Unpaid leave doesn't help low-wage workers much
Warrant: Many part-time workers can't afford unpaid time off.
Impact: Paid leave policies might do more good.

## Likely questions
### Does this change which employers must follow FMLA?
- For: either
Answer: No. It changes worker eligibility; the 50-employee rule for employers stays the same.

### Why 90 days instead of six months?
- For: aff
Answer: It matches common probation periods and covers seasonal and part-time workers.

### If leave is unpaid, how much does this really help?
- For: aff
Answer: Job protection itself matters: workers can recover without losing their job.

### Won't businesses avoid hiring part-time workers?
- For: aff
Answer: Small businesses under 50 employees are already exempt.
