# AI Learning Aid: A Bill to Lower the Federal Corporate Tax Rate to 15% for Domestic Manufacturers

## Summary
Lowers the federal corporate tax rate from 21% to 15% for C corporations that manufacture in the United States, meaning they turn raw materials into finished products at U.S. plants.

## Affirmative arguments
### Lower taxes bring manufacturing home
Warrant: A lower rate makes U.S. factories more competitive with other countries.
Impact: More domestic jobs and stronger supply chains.

### The 2017 tax cut boosted investment
Warrant: Research found investment rose at firms that got larger tax cuts.
Impact: A targeted cut can do the same for manufacturing.

### Supply chains are a national security issue
Warrant: The pandemic showed the risk of relying on foreign factories.
Impact: Domestic production protects the country in a crisis.

## Negative arguments
### It adds to the deficit
Warrant: Studies found the 2017 cut did not pay for itself.
Impact: Larger deficits burden future generations.

### Benefits go to shareholders
Warrant: Much of the 2017 cut went to stock buybacks and dividends.
Impact: Workers may see little benefit.

### Defining 'manufacturing' invites loopholes
Warrant: Companies will relabel activities to qualify.
Impact: The tax code gets more complicated and less fair.

## Likely questions
### How much will this cost in lost revenue?
- For: aff
Answer: Be ready with an estimate or argue growth offsets part of it.

### What stops a company from calling itself a manufacturer?
- For: aff
Answer: The bill defines manufacturing as turning raw materials into finished goods at U.S. plants.

### Did the 2017 cut raise workers' wages?
- For: neg
Answer: Neg should say wage gains were smaller than promised.

### Why only C corporations?
- For: either
Answer: Other businesses pay through individual tax rates, not the corporate rate.
