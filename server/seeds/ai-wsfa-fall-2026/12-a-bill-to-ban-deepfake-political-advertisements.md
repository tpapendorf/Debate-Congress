# AI Learning Aid: A Bill to Ban Deepfake Political Advertisements

## Summary
Bans deepfake political ads, meaning AI or other manipulated images, video or audio made knowingly to spread false information about a candidate, when used to influence federal elections. The FEC would enforce it with penalties for each violation.

## Affirmative arguments
### Deepfakes can deceive voters at scale
Warrant: AI makes realistic fake video and audio cheap and fast to produce.
Impact: A last-minute fake can change an election before it is debunked.

### The bill is narrow
Warrant: It covers only knowingly false content about candidates in federal election ads.
Impact: It targets lies, not satire or opinion.

### Trust in elections depends on real information
Warrant: When voters can't tell what's real, they stop trusting anything.
Impact: Protecting elections protects democracy.

## Negative arguments
### Courts have struck down similar laws
Warrant: Judges have blocked state deepfake election laws on First Amendment grounds.
Impact: A federal ban would likely face the same fate.

### Who decides what's 'misleading'?
Warrant: Political speech is often exaggerated, and enforcement could be used against opponents.
Impact: It could chill parody and legitimate criticism.

### Existing laws already cover fraud and defamation
Warrant: Candidates can sue for defamation and platforms label AI content.
Impact: A new ban adds little.

## Likely questions
### How would this bill survive the First Amendment when state laws haven't?
- For: aff
Answer: It's narrower: only knowing falsehoods about candidates in ads, which courts treat like fraud.

### Does it ban parody or satire?
- For: aff
Answer: No, the intent to spread false information is required.

### Who proves the creator knew it was fake?
- For: aff
Answer: The FEC, using evidence like how the content was made.

### Would a candidate's own AI-generated ad be banned?
- For: either
Answer: Only if it spreads false information about another candidate.
