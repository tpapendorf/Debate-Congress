# AI Learning Aid: The Get the FLOCK Out Act

## Summary
Withholds 10% of a state's federal highway funding each year unless the state bans automated license plate readers (ALPRs) except for tolls, stolen vehicles and missing or endangered people. It takes effect in fiscal year 2028.

## Affirmative arguments
### License plate readers create mass surveillance
Warrant: Cameras record the movements of millions of drivers who are not suspected of anything.
Impact: That data can show where people live, worship and get medical care.

### Data is shared beyond local police
Warrant: Reports show ALPR data reaching federal agencies like ICE without direct agreements.
Impact: Local communities lose control of how their data is used.

### Cities are already backing away
Warrant: Many cities have canceled ALPR contracts over privacy concerns.
Impact: A national standard protects everyone.

## Negative arguments
### ALPRs help solve serious crimes
Warrant: Police use plate readers to find suspects in shootings, kidnappings and hit-and-runs.
Impact: Banning them would let crimes go unsolved.

### Withholding highway money is coercive
Warrant: Using road funding to force state laws punishes drivers with worse roads.
Impact: It may also face legal challenge as coercion.

### States should set their own rules
Warrant: States can limit data retention and sharing instead of banning the tool.
Impact: Targeted rules protect privacy without losing benefits.

## Likely questions
### Why allow stolen vehicles and missing persons but not murder suspects?
- For: aff
Answer: Those uses are narrow and time-sensitive; aff can accept amendments.

### Is withholding highway funds constitutional?
- For: aff
Answer: Yes, Congress used the same method to set the national drinking age.

### Couldn't states just limit how long data is kept?
- For: aff
Answer: Limits haven't stopped data from being shared with other agencies.

### How many crimes have ALPRs helped solve?
- For: neg
Answer: Neg should have examples ready; aff should ask for data on misuse.
