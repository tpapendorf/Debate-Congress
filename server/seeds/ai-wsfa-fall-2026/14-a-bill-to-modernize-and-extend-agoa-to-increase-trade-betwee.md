# AI Learning Aid: A Bill to Modernize and Extend AGOA to Increase Trade Between the United States and Africa

## Summary
Extends the African Growth and Opportunity Act through 2041 for long-term stability, and spends $250 million a year on a USTR-run trade capacity initiative to help African businesses meet U.S. market requirements.

## Affirmative arguments
### AGOA supports jobs in Africa and the U.S.
Warrant: Duty-free access built industries like apparel and auto parts in eligible countries.
Impact: Stable trade lifts people out of poverty and creates U.S. export markets.

### Short renewals scare off investors
Warrant: Factories need years of certainty before they invest.
Impact: A 15-year extension brings lasting investment.

### It counters China's influence
Warrant: China is Africa's largest trading partner.
Impact: Stronger U.S. trade ties build partnerships and security.

## Negative arguments
### Benefits are concentrated in a few countries
Warrant: Much AGOA trade comes from oil and a handful of nations.
Impact: The program doesn't reach most of Africa.

### $250 million a year is costly
Warrant: Taxpayers pay for capacity building abroad.
Impact: Money could go to U.S. workers instead.

### Long extensions remove leverage
Warrant: Periodic renewal lets the U.S. push for reforms like human rights and fair trade.
Impact: A 2041 date weakens accountability.
