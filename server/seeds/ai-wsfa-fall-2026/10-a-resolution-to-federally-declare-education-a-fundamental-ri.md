# AI Learning Aid: A Resolution to Federally Declare Education a Fundamental Right

## Summary
Recommends that the federal government declare education a fundamental right and increase federal funding and involvement in education, citing unequal school funding tied to local property taxes.

## Affirmative arguments
### School funding depends on zip code
Warrant: Relying on local property taxes gives wealthy districts far more money per student.
Impact: Students in poor areas get fewer opportunities.

### The Supreme Court left a gap
Warrant: In San Antonio v. Rodriguez (1973) the Court ruled education is not a fundamental right under the Constitution.
Impact: Without that status, federal courts can't fix unequal funding.

### Education drives economic mobility
Warrant: Quality schools are the main path out of poverty.
Impact: Equal access strengthens the whole economy.

## Negative arguments
### Education is a state responsibility
Warrant: States run and fund most of education, and nearly all state constitutions already guarantee it.
Impact: Federal control could reduce local flexibility.

### Money alone doesn't guarantee results
Warrant: How funds are spent matters as much as how much.
Impact: More federal dollars may not improve outcomes.

### Courts are a poor tool for school policy
Warrant: Turning education into a federal right invites decades of lawsuits.
Impact: Legislatures, not judges, should set budgets.

## Likely questions
### What would a fundamental right to education actually guarantee?
- For: aff
Answer: A minimum standard of quality and equal funding, enforceable in court.

### Doesn't every state constitution already protect education?
- For: aff
Answer: State protections vary widely and haven't closed the funding gaps.

### Where would the extra federal money come from?
- For: aff
Answer: Be ready with a funding source or admit it's a priority choice.

### Would this mean federal control of curriculum?
- For: aff
Answer: No, it's about funding and access, not what's taught.
