# AI Learning Aid: A Bill to Restrict the Levying of Tariffs on Canada

## Summary
Prohibits the executive branch from putting tariffs on Canada without prior approval from Congress, which holds the taxing power in Article I, Section 8. Officials who violate it could be impeached and the tariffs struck down.

## Affirmative arguments
### The Constitution gives Congress the power to tax
Warrant: Tariffs are taxes, and Article I gives that power to Congress.
Impact: Restoring the check protects the separation of powers.

### Tariffs on Canada hurt Americans
Warrant: Canada is one of the largest U.S. trading partners, and tariffs raise prices on goods like lumber, energy and cars.
Impact: Consumers and manufacturers pay more.

### The Supreme Court has already limited emergency tariffs
Warrant: In 2026 the Court struck down tariffs imposed under the emergency powers law.
Impact: Congress should write the rule clearly.

## Negative arguments
### The President needs flexibility
Warrant: Trade disputes can require quick action.
Impact: Waiting for Congress weakens U.S. leverage.

### Singling out Canada is odd
Warrant: Why protect one country and not others?
Impact: Trade policy should be consistent.

### Impeachment is an extreme remedy
Warrant: Using impeachment for trade decisions politicizes the process.
Impact: Courts and normal oversight are better tools.

## Likely questions
### Why only Canada and not all countries?
- For: aff
Answer: Canada is a close ally and top partner, and tariffs on it hurt U.S. industries most.

### What if the President needs to respond to an emergency?
- For: aff
Answer: Congress can act quickly when needed, as it does for other emergencies.

### Isn't impeachment too extreme for a tariff?
- For: aff
Answer: It's the Constitution's tool for officials who ignore the law.

### Didn't the Supreme Court already solve this?
- For: neg
Answer: It only covered emergency-power tariffs; other statutes remain.
