# AI Learning Aid: A Bill to Restore Union Rights

## Summary
Amends the Taft-Hartley Act to let unions use secondary boycotts and repeals Section 14(b), which ends state right-to-work laws. The Department of Labor would enforce it 50 days after passage.

## Affirmative arguments
### Right-to-work laws weaken unions and lower wages
Warrant: Workers can get union benefits without paying dues, which drains union resources.
Impact: Weaker unions mean lower pay and benefits.

### Secondary boycotts restore workers' bargaining power
Warrant: Companies can shift work to subcontractors to avoid strikes.
Impact: Allowing pressure on business partners balances power.

### Union membership is near historic lows
Warrant: Legal limits since 1947 have made organizing harder.
Impact: Restoring rights helps rebuild the middle class.

## Negative arguments
### Workers shouldn't be forced to pay a union
Warrant: Right-to-work protects individual choice.
Impact: Repeal forces dues on workers who disagree.

### States should decide
Warrant: Twenty-six states have chosen right-to-work laws.
Impact: Overriding them ignores voters' choices.

### Secondary boycotts hurt neutral businesses
Warrant: They pull companies not part of a dispute into strikes.
Impact: Supply chains and consumers suffer.

## Likely questions
### Should a worker who disagrees with the union have to pay it?
- For: aff
Answer: They get the contract's raises and protections, so they should share the cost.

### Why should Congress override 26 states?
- For: aff
Answer: Labor law is national; workers' rights shouldn't depend on their state.

### How does a secondary boycott affect a business with no dispute?
- For: aff
Answer: It's limited pressure on companies doing business with the employer, as was legal before 1947.

### Do right-to-work states attract more jobs?
- For: neg
Answer: Neg can argue businesses prefer them; aff should answer with wage data.
