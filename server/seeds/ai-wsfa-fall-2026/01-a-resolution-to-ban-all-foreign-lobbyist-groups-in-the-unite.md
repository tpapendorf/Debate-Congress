# AI Learning Aid: A Resolution to Ban All Foreign Lobbyist Groups in The United States

## Summary
Bans foreign lobbying groups from lobbying the U.S. government for foreign governments, political parties or other foreign principals. Today the Foreign Agents Registration Act (FARA) mostly requires these agents to register and disclose their work rather than banning it.

## Affirmative arguments
### Disclosure has not stopped foreign money from shaping U.S. policy
Warrant: Foreign governments and companies spend heavily on registered agents, and disclosure filings are late, incomplete and rarely read by voters.
Impact: Policy can tilt toward the interests of whoever pays most, not toward Americans.

### A ban removes conflicts of interest for former officials
Warrant: Many foreign lobbyists are former members of Congress and staff who sell their access.
Impact: Ending the market for that access protects trust in government.

### FARA enforcement is weak
Warrant: The Justice Department has historically prosecuted few FARA cases, so the disclosure rules lack teeth.
Impact: A clear ban is simpler to enforce than a paperwork rule.

## Negative arguments
### A total ban likely violates the First Amendment
Warrant: Lobbying is a form of petitioning the government, and courts protect speech even when foreign-linked groups fund it.
Impact: The law could be struck down, leaving nothing in place.

### Allies lose a legitimate voice
Warrant: Countries like Ukraine, Taiwan and Israel use lobbyists to explain their needs to Congress.
Impact: Cutting them off could weaken U.S. alliances and diplomacy.

### Influence would move underground
Warrant: A ban gives foreign actors a reason to use unregistered intermediaries instead of disclosed lobbyists.
Impact: We would lose the transparency FARA provides today.

## Likely questions
### Would a U.S. company owned by a foreign parent be banned from lobbying?
- For: neg
Answer: The resolution covers lobbying for foreign principals; supporters should say where they draw the line.

### How is a ban different from what FARA already requires?
- For: aff
Answer: FARA only requires registration and disclosure; this would stop the lobbying itself.

### Doesn't the right to petition the government protect this speech?
- For: aff
Answer: Congress can regulate foreign governments' influence more strictly than citizens' speech, but be ready for court challenges.

### How would our allies make their case to Congress?
- For: aff
Answer: Through embassies and official diplomacy rather than paid lobbying firms.
